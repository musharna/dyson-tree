## 1. Concept and origin

Freeman Dyson introduced the idea of a genetically engineered plant living on a comet in the 1972 Bernal Lecture ("The World, the Flesh and the Devil", Birkbeck College), developed it in *Disturbing the Universe* (1979, chapter "The Greening of the Galaxy"), and restated it for a general audience in *The Atlantic* (Nov 1997, "Warm-Blooded Plants and Freeze-Dried Fish") `[S]`.

The consensus definition (Wikipedia, "Dyson tree") is "a hypothetical genetically engineered plant (perhaps resembling a tree) capable of growing inside a comet", with the mature form "forming a spherical structure possibly dozens of kilometers across" `[S]`. The thermal-regulation premise is usually attributed to existing thermogenic plants (skunk cabbage, titan arum) `[S]`; that attribution is a modern gloss, not something I can place in Dyson's text.

**unknown:** Dyson's own verbatim wording. Direct fetches of the Atlantic essay and its Wayback copy were blocked this session, so no sentence here is quoted from Dyson. Anything attributed to him above is secondary. Re-fetch after the WebFetch limit resets (midnight America/New_York) before quoting him in any pre-registration.

Design-relevant reading of the concept: the tree is simultaneously (a) a photosynthetic organism at outer-system light levels, (b) a self-built pressure vessel holding an atmosphere against vacuum, and (c) a mining/recycling loop feeding on cometary volatiles and dust. Sections 3–7 test each of those against measured biology and comet chemistry.

## 2. Prior art on the Dyson tree itself

**Academic literature on the label: effectively nil.** OpenAlex exact-phrase search for "Dyson tree" returns 12 works, of which one is relevant and only mentions it: Das Gupta 2020 (arXiv 2010.07767) notes that "Dyson's futuristic ideas concerning … Dyson tree, Dyson sphere and so on, have also been discussed briefly" `[Q]`. No paper models, sizes, or tests the organism.

**Fiction and games** (Wikipedia list, `[S]`): Pollack 1984, Niven *The Integral Trees* 1984, Sagan & Druyan *Comet* 1985, Swanwick *Vacuum Flowers* 1987, Moffitt, Simmons *Hyperion* 1996/97 (Ousters), Baxter 2001, Orion's Arm, Transhuman Space, Eclipse Phase 2e, Tenchi Muyo, Dirty Pair, and the game *Eufloria* (named after the hypothesis; seedlings planted on asteroids, tree count capped per asteroid). None carries a physiological model; they are name-level prior art only.

**Null re-run routed to the biology, not the label** (the bust flagged in the roadmap). OpenAlex query "genetically engineered plants comet Kuiper belt habitat" returns 2 works (Romanovskaya 2023, 10.1017/s1473550423000204; Vidal 2026, arXiv 2605.21093), neither a comet-scale organism. The nearest real prior art is:

- **Grown habitats (myco-architecture).** Brandić Lipińska 2022 (10.3389/fbuil.2022.965145): "fungal bio-composites for growing extra-terrestrial structures, directly at the destination" `[Q]`; Brandić Lipińska 2023 "Drop the Base" (10.32865/2346/94678, with Rothschild/NASA Ames); Santomartino 2023 (10.1038/s41467-023-37070-2); Koehle 2023 (10.1038/s41526-023-00285-0).
- **Mechanistic BLSS crop models.** Poulet 2016 (10.1080/23818107.2016.1194228): "including mechanistic modelling for future space gardeners" `[Q]`; De Micco 2023 review (10.1038/s41526-023-00317-9); NASA BVAD 2018 (hdl 2060/20180001338).

Conclusion: no one has built or modelled the organism. The closest live fields are bio-composite structures and closed-loop crop models, both Earth-pressure, Earth-light, and not self-supporting in vacuum. This project's simulation would be first-of-kind on the *label* and would JOIN those two fields on the *biology*.

## 3. Cometary element budget

**README premise under test:** "CHON-rich, P/K/metal-poor". The evidence splits it.

*CHON-rich: supported.* Rosetta/COSIMA at 67P: dust has a "high carbon content (atomic C/Si = 5.5 +1.4/−1.2 on average) close to the solar value and comparable to the 1P/Halley data" `[Q]` and is "nearly 50 per cent organic matter in mass, mixed with mineral phases that are mostly anhydrous" `[Q]` (Bardyn 2017, 10.1093/mnras/stx2640). Hänni 2025 (10.1051/0004-6361/202554563) confirms a "surprisingly large portion of organics, both in the refractory and the icy phases" `[Q]`.

*P-poor: contradicted.* ROSINA detected "volatile glycine accompanied by methylamine and ethylamine in the coma" `[Q]` "together with the detection of phosphorus and a multitude of organic molecules" `[Q]` (Altwegg 2016, 10.1126/sciadv.1600285). P is present; its abundance relative to plant demand is **unknown** (no quantified P/Si found).

*Metal-poor: contradicted for the refractory fraction.* Stardust samples from 81P/Wild 2 are "within a factor of 1.22 bulk CI chondritic element ratios relative to Fe for Na, Mg, Al, Ti, Cr, Mn, and Co" `[Q]`, with "depletion in Ca by a factor of four and an enrichment in Ni by a factor of two" `[Q]` (Stephan 2008, 10.1111/j.1945-5100.2008.tb00619.x). So the mineral half of the dust is roughly chondritic in Mg/Fe/Mn etc. The dust-to-ice ratio itself is poorly constrained: reviews show "wide ranges of plausible values" `[Q]` (Choukroun 2020, 10.1007/s11214-020-00662-1). Halley context: Jessberger 1988 (Nature, 10.1038/332691a0; no abstract, cited via Bardyn's comparison) and Jessberger & Kissel 1991 (10.1017/s0252921100012823).

*K-poor:* **unknown.** No cometary potassium measurement found in this sweep either way.

*Mechanical substrate.* 67P material has "shear modulus μ of 3.6 MPa ≤ μ ≤ 346 MPa, and a Young's modulus E of 7.2 MPa ≤ E ≤ 980 MPa" `[Q]` (Knapmeyer 2018, 10.1016/j.icarus.2017.12.002). That range overlaps plant tissue (Section 4), which matters for anchoring.

**Action for the README:** rewrite the premise as "organic-C-rich, refractory fraction near-chondritic, Ca-depleted; P present, K unmeasured". Full volatile inventory: Altwegg 2019 review (10.1146/annurev-astro-091918-104409).

## 4. Biological pressure vessel

Can plant tissue hold an internal atmosphere against vacuum? Two sub-questions: material strength, and what pressure the physiology actually needs.

*Material strength.* Across plant tissues "Young's modulus varies from 0.3 MPa in parenchyma to 30 GPa in the densest palm, while the compressive strength varies from 0.3 MPa in parenchyma to over 300 MPa in dense palm" `[Q]` (Gibson 2012, 10.1098/rsif.2012.0341). At the cell scale the "wood cell wall … acts as a pressure vessel" `[Q]` (Zhang 2015, 10.1088/0965-0393/23/8/085010); hierarchy review in Jakob 2021 (10.1016/j.pmatsci.2021.100916). **unknown:** a quotable bulk *tensile* strength for wood; use Gibson's compressive range only as an order-of-magnitude proxy, or source a tensile number before any wall-thickness calculation.

*Required pressure.* Plants do not need 101 kPa. In Arabidopsis "atmosphere of 10 kPa compared with the sea-level pressure of 101 kPa resulted in the significant differential expression of more than 200 genes" `[Q]`, and "water movement is a paramount issue at low atmospheric pressures" `[Q]` (Paul 2004, 10.1104/pp.103.032607). The same paper states the engineering motive: "severe limitations to producing … plant growth facilities that contain Earth-normal atmospheric pressures within light, transparent structures" `[Q]`. Zhou 2017 (10.3389/fpls.2017.00528) factorially varied 97/50/25 kPa against pO2 21/10/5 kPa and found "adding back oxygen to normoxic conditions did not completely alleviate gene expression changes" `[Q]`, so hypobaria is not purely a hypoxia effect. Current flight hardware "operate at 1 atm (101 kPa) pressure" `[Q]` (Stutte 2022, 10.1016/j.lssr.2022.06.001); He 2013 (10.1007/s11099-013-0047-9) covers photosynthesis under hypobaria.

*Derived sizing* `[D]`: thin-wall sphere hoop stress σ = p·r/(2t). At p = 10 kPa and r = 10 km, σ·t = 5×10⁷ N/m; against a 300 MPa dense-tissue ceiling that is t ≈ 0.17 m of the strongest tissue, or tens of metres of parenchyma-grade material. Sensitive to the tensile number that is still unknown.

## 5. Light compensation point under 1/r²

*Solar input.* "Total solar irradiance during the 2008 solar minimum period is 1360.8 ± 0.5 W m−2" `[Q]` (Kopp & Lean 2011, 10.1029/2010GL045777). Derived `[D]`: assuming ~45% of TSI is PAR at 4.57 µmol photons/J, that is ~2000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at 1 AU, ~20 at 10 AU, ~1.25 at 40 AU.

*Plant floor.* Across 115 species, shade-tolerant seedlings reach "positive carbon balance at light levels approximately 3 μmol m−2 s−1 less … (LSMs = 5.3 vs 8.1, 9.0 μmol m−2 s−1)" `[Q]` and "over 90% of the time light levels were below 20 μmol m−2 s−1" `[Q]` in their native understory (Craine & Reich 2005, 10.1111/j.1469-8137.2005.01420.x). Meta-analysis of "500 experiments with 760 plant species" `[Q]` spans a daily light integral of "1–50 mol m−2 d−1" `[Q]` (Poorter 2019, 10.1111/nph.15754). Whole-plant framing: Givnish 1988 (10.1071/pp9880063).

*Algal floor.* "Growth for some species has been reported at less than 1 μE m−1 s−2" `[Q]` [sic] (Richardson 1983, 10.1111/j.1469-8137.1983.tb03422.x); polar phytoplankton PSII function under extreme low light in Poirier 2026 (10.3389/fphbi.2026.1787189).

*Crossover* `[D]`: a vascular-plant LCP of 5–9 µmol is met out to roughly 15–20 AU; an algal floor near 1 µmol reaches ~45 AU. Kuiper-belt (30–50 AU) operation therefore needs either algal-grade photosystems, light concentration, or a non-photosynthetic energy budget. Temperature acclimation of photosynthesis (Hikosaka 2006, 10.1093/jxb/erj049) is a separate axis not yet sized.

**unknown:** published outer-system PAR values; Cockell & Raven 2009 (10.1080/17550870903329328) "calculated photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) fluxes on a diversity of planetary bodies" `[Q]` but the abstract gives none, so the 1/r² numbers above are mine.

## 6. Spaceflight and fractional-gravity plant biology

*GeneLab as the gate.* Barker 2023 (10.1038/s41526-023-00247-6) "compared transcriptomic analysis of 15 Arabidopsis thaliana spaceflight experiments deposited in … GeneLab" `[Q]` and warns that "analysis type (i.e., microarray versus RNA-seq) or environmental and hardware conditions have important confounding effects" `[Q]`. Any physiology-C validation against GeneLab must control platform and hardware, not pool datasets. Tooling: TOAST (Barker 2020, 10.3389/fpls.2020.00147); alt-splicing reanalyses of OSD-37/38/120 (Gupta & Jaiswal, Zenodo 13180907, 17438022, 13181441, CC-BY-SA 4.0); Shahbazi 2024 (10.1007/s11103-024-01478-1).

*Gravity sensing.* In spaceflight roots "displayed typically 'vertical' distribution of auxin in the primary root" `[Q]`; the "distribution of auxin in the gravity-sensing portion of the root is not dependent on gravity" `[Q]` (Ferl & Paul 2016, 10.1038/npjmgrav.2015.23). Fractional gravity: Herranz 2019 (10.3389/fpls.2019.01529) tested "micro-g, several intermediate partial" `[Q]` levels over 6 days; Kiss 2012 (Planta 236:635–645, 10.1007/s00425-012-1633-y) is closed with no abstract, content **unknown** beyond title; Kiss 2019 (10.3389/fpls.2019.01577); Manzano 2018 (10.1038/s41526-018-0041-4).

*Transcriptome and stress.* Paul 2013 (10.1186/1471-2229-13-112), Paul 2017 (10.1371/journal.pone.0180186), Jost 2015 (10.3390/plants4010044); reviews Medina 2021 (10.3389/fspas.2021.729154), De Micco 2023 (10.1038/s41526-023-00315-x), Maffei 2024 (10.1038/s42003-024-06989-7).

Design implication: gravitropism can be dropped from the model without contradicting data (auxin patterning persists without g), but every other spaceflight response is confounded by hardware and must be represented as a hardware-conditioned prior, not a fixed physiology.

## 7. Stress hardening: cold, vacuum, radiation

*Vacuum + UV survival exists at the seed stage.* "Seeds exposed for 558 days outside the International Space Station (ISS) germinated and developed into fertile plants" `[Q]`; on EXPOSE-R this was "nearly lethal exposure" `[Q]`, with "UV254nm … 2420 MJ m−2. Morning glory seeds resisted this maximum dose, which killed tobacco and Arabidopsis" `[Q]` (Tepfer 2017, 10.1089/ast.2015.1457). Follow-on seed exposure: Richards 2025 MISSE-Seed (10.1038/s41526-024-00451-y).

*Whole-organism exposure is lichen, not plant.* Xanthoria elegans gave the "first long-term exposure of eukaryotic organisms to the hostile space conditions of the low Earth orbit" `[Q]` at 1.5 years (Brandt 2014, 10.1017/s1473550414000214); de la Torre 2010 (10.1016/j.icarus.2010.03.010). No vegetative vascular plant has survived vacuum exposure in any source found.

*Ionising radiation.* "Effects of IR on plants are understood best at acute high doses" `[Q]` with "few controlled experiments that used chronic low doses" `[Q]` (Caplin & Willey 2018, 10.3389/fpls.2018.00847). Acute references: Arabidopsis 200 Gy (Kim 2007, 10.1667/rr0963.1); rice 100–300 Gy (Choi 2021, 10.3390/plants10030439); Laanen 2021 review (10.3389/fpls.2021.611783). The chronic outer-system dose regime is exactly the under-studied one.

*Cold:* **unknown** at cometary temperatures; nothing in this sweep addresses metabolism below roughly −40 °C for plants, and the thermogenic-plant premise (Section 1) has no comet-relevant heat-budget source.

## 8. What this means for the science spine

Which README premises hold, sorted by evidential status:

| Premise | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Comet is CHON/organic-rich | holds | Bardyn 2017, Hänni 2025 |
| Comet is P-poor | contradicted (P detected, abundance unknown) | Altwegg 2016 |
| Comet is metal-poor | contradicted for refractory fraction (near-CI) | Stephan 2008 |
| Comet is K-poor | unknown | no source |
| Tissue can hold ~10 kPa | plausible, tensile number missing | Gibson 2012, Paul 2004 |
| 10 kPa is physiologically viable | strained: >200 DEGs, water transport limiting | Paul 2004, Zhou 2017 |
| LCP met at Kuiper distances | fails for vascular LCP; marginal for algal floor | Craine & Reich 2005, Richardson 1983 |
| GeneLab can validate physiology C | yes, if hardware-conditioned | Barker 2023 |
| Gravitropism needed | no; auxin patterning is g-independent | Ferl & Paul 2016 |
| Vegetative vacuum survival | no precedent; seeds and lichens only | Tepfer 2017, Brandt 2014 |
| Chronic low-dose radiation response | under-studied | Caplin & Willey 2018 |

Two items change the design space rather than a parameter: the light floor (Section 5) forces a choice between algal photosystems, concentration, or a chemotrophic budget before any colony loop is meaningful; and the comet composition rewrite (Section 3) removes "metal scarcity" as a free constraint, so the element-budget module cannot lean on it for emergent failures.

Open decision A (designer loop) vs B (colony loop) is not resolved by this survey, but B inherits the light-floor problem in full, while A can treat it as a fixed input.
